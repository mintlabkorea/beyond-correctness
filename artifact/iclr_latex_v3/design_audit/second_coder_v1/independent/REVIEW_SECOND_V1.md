# Independent second design coding, v1

All 25 supplied comparison rows were retained and independently coded. No primary design judgments, extracted outcome artifact, project memory, or files outside the packet and this coder's output directory were consulted. No external search or additional agents were used. The source and scope evidence came from the supplied factual sheet and construction-focused inspection of packet source texts. Titles/authors were checked in each main source: Dinh et al. (LIFT), Hegselmann et al. (TabLLM), Ruiz et al. (PLATO), Kim et al. (CARTE), Han et al. (FeatLLM), Gardner et al. (TabuLa), Spinaci et al. (ConTextTab), Arazi et al. (TabSTAR), and Kim/Lefebvre/Brison (TARTE mirror). Packet-file hashes were checked against the manifest. The source texts were the supplied layout extractions; no runtime implementations were inspected or rerun.

The 25 scope declarations were written to SCOPES_SECOND_V1.json and hashed to SCOPES_SECOND_V1.sha256 before the gate-assignment script was written or run. The scope file contains no gates. Gate assignments then copied those declarations without alteration. Scope declaration is not a preregistration and did not precede reading the construction. The final manifest binds the scope, its first hash, the gate codes, this review, and the local scripts and verification report.

Reported outcomes were not extracted or used in assigning design codes. Literal outcome blindness is not claimed: full-paper search matches and neighboring text incidentally exposed numerical results, tables, and authors' effect statements. These were disregarded for the gates. In particular, a result or plotted curve was never used as proof of correct execution or interface compatibility.

The final admissibility counts are 5 Yes, 9 No, and 11 Unclear. These count comparisons, not independent experiments or studies. No significance, effect direction, effect magnitude, or supported-claim result has been extracted. Gate codes are relative to the frozen declared scopes, not global judgments about papers.

## Scope decisions

For shuffle interventions the tested use is meaningful content supplied at the relevant input channel. Wrong associations therefore do not become semantic absence by defining the scope as 'correct meaning.' Continuous values and categories retain their status as protected observations even when a semantic representation is under test. A shared fixed permutation can preserve observations, whereas binning cannot preserve their original precision.

TabLLM's ordered value list protects feature slots and boundaries but does not separately protect the removed name prefix. That leads to an admissible name-removal reference under this scope. By contrast, LIFT's two named formats have a within-format scaffold and a changed category/output representation that is not completely documented. Alternative, more permissive scaffold choices could change a preservation assessment; no extra rows were created to explore them.

For PLATO, the first-layer inference scaffold is protected. No KG becomes a conventional MLP and thus fails Preservation while passing Interface: a model can be a valid predictor yet differ in a protected component. The feature-only KG asks about broader-domain information across both graph processing and pretrained embeddings, so unclear recomputation limits Removal. The 50% edge row is assessed against the entire edge-information use, which demonstrably persists. I did not redefine that row as withdrawal of the realized deleted subset.

FeatLLM's explanatory definitions are the tested semantic component, while explicit types and categorical-domain examples are protected schema metadata. Removing the whole description block withdraws both. A broader scope that intentionally includes structural metadata would ask a different question. Newly generated rules and binary features themselves are allowed intervention consequences.

ConTextTab and CARTE encoder changes are not automatic failures. The external pretrained linguistic component can be absent while lexical string structure remains. However, the reporting does not establish all nonsemantic distinctions, numeric/missingness handling or per-encoder adaptation. For AutoGluon, the generic encoder name leaves its configured text handling insufficiently specified even for complete Removal. ConTextTab's generic architecture admits learned dimension projection, which makes compatible adaptation plausible but is not a per-arm implementation certificate.

TabSTAR scopes concern explicit wording in its semantic branch. Numeric information retained through its separate numerical branch is protected evidence preservation, not a Removal failure. The source explicitly provides a common variant experiment in §6; refitting/pretraining state changes under that recipe are allowed.

For TARTE's compound MinHash/random-weights row, table pretraining is protected separately from string-encoder meaning. The published labels establish the extra pretraining change. No new contrast against a FastText/random-weights arm was invented.

## Source checks and factual-sheet qualifications

- LIFT: D.2.1 independently confirms repeated 'semester' in Shuffled-Names I and changed/repeated values in Shuffled-Names II. An additional incompleteness is that Correct-Names II itself omits the class-size value present in Correct-Names I and the unnamed example. This reinforces uncertainty about illustrative templates versus executed templates; it is not proof of actual experimental data deletion. The supplement offers one unnamed example while Table 9 names two variants.
- TabLLM: §3.2 explicitly confirms common permutation across examples and ten uniform bins for continuous values. The list-only operation retains ordered values rather than replacing feature labels with generic names. The factual sheet is consistent with these statements.
- PLATO: the correct source is Ruiz et al., and §§3.3–3.5 distinguish KG pretraining from supervised message passing. Eq.(1) and the fixed-embedding statement make embedding provenance material for the feature-only contrast. The factual sheet correctly leaves contrast-specific recomputation unresolved.
- CARTE: Figure 1 explicitly multiplies a numerical value by its corresponding column feature. C.3 gives the MinHash string n-gram substitution but does not operationalize the edge-exclusion labels. Thus a figure label cannot establish that every use of column semantics is absent.
- FeatLLM: §3.1 and Appendix A.2 confirm that descriptions bundle explanatory and structural information. The exact ablated prompt and per-arm parser recovery are unavailable in the reviewed construction.
- TabuLa: F.2 explicitly replaces both feature and target headers and says data are unaltered. The scope includes both header classes.
- ConTextTab: §3.1 provides modality-specific paths and dimension projection; §5.1 names the four encoder alternatives, indexed header replacement, and five-row description generation. Unspecified row provenance is uncertainty about information matching, not evidence of leakage.
- TabSTAR: §6's opening experiment description states that each variant is pretrained on a common 256-dataset regime and evaluated on the remaining 20 datasets. The factual sheet's statement that exact per-variant checkpoints are not detailed is literally compatible with this, but should not be interpreted as absence of a common training/evaluation recipe. Table 31 confirms the generic Numeric marker and missing-value illustrations. Identical checkpoints are neither expected nor required under the rule.
- TARTE: only the author mirror was inspected directly, and its first page identifies arXiv:2505.14415v2, 30 June 2025. Its Figure 6 corroborates comparator labels and Ridge evaluation. It is preacceptance material, not the accepted published PDF. Published construction is available here only through the supplied factual sheet's record of indexed Figure 6/§4.3; mirror-only implementation details were not promoted to published facts. Source access is a real limit on independent verification for these two rows.

No factual-sheet edits were made. The additional LIFT omission and TabSTAR common-recipe clarification are qualifications for subsequent review, not revisions to this frozen independent coding. Missing reporting received Unclear where material; no Fail was assigned solely from missing detail, architecture change alone, or lack of identical fitted weights.
