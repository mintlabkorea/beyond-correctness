\section{Semantics-native model-coverage extensions}
\label{sec:app-learner-coverage}
\label{app:semantic_learners}

TransTab and CARTE are \emph{post-result model-coverage extensions}, not
members of the predictor set fixed before execution, and their evidence is not
uniform. The TransTab four-condition extension attributes the conventional gap
to shared identifiers and worse performance under the wrong condition rather
than to positive name sensitivity beyond those identifiers. CARTE supplies
qualified $K=512$ content sensitivity without uniform predictive utility or a
relation replication.

For TransTab, other semantic knowledge is held in the intended condition while
correspondence knowledge varies. The four conditions use intended names, anonymous
identifiers shared across corresponding source/target columns, stable anonymous
identifiers with distinct domain-local namespaces, and wrong names. The
distinct-anonymous reference preserves within-domain column identity and
source transfer while removing shared cross-domain identifiers; it
is not target-only. 
The reference intentionally removes transfer mediated by shared cross-domain
identifier identity, which is the tested semantic knowledge. Source rows,
within-domain identities, model parameterization, and all other training paths
are unchanged. Because the reference uses a larger identifier vocabulary than
the shared-identifier condition, it does not have fewer embedding parameters,
so its lower score cannot be attributed to reduced capacity.
At $K=32$, the shared-identifier condition exceeds the reference by
$+.114$--$+.129$, while $Q_{\mathrm{wrong}}-Q_{\mathrm{reference}}$ is
$-.456$--$-.361$; worse performance under the wrong condition accounts for
74--80\% of the conventional intended--wrong gap. At $K=512$, the
same shared-identifier difference attenuates to $+.007$--$+.009$, and worse
performance under the wrong condition accounts for 97--100\% of the gap.
The intended-minus-shared-identifier difference has no positive interval in any
of the six family--budget strata and is negative in three. Exact decomposition
residuals are below $1.2\times10^{-16}$.

The authoritative official-source runs use Transformers 4.30.0.  For the
earlier three-condition compatibility grid, an audit against 5.12.0 exactly matched
all 360 normalized-MSE floats, 1,080 construction hashes, and prespecified
contrasts; the new four-condition grid was refit only under the authoritative
environment.  Because the regression head postdates the
original TransTab paper and the model in the four-condition analysis is initialized from scratch,
the name/token contrast is not an identification of pretrained language
semantics; tokenization, surface statistics, and optimization remain possible
causes.  A pretrained schema-token encoder could in principle produce a
positive name/token contrast; this experiment instead identifies the
contribution of shared tokens in the scratch-initialized model as shared
identity rather than lexical semantic knowledge. Compatibility details are in the
artifact README.

CARTE receives the full M/C/R condition set fixed before execution. Its
correspondence knowledge slice is inconclusive at $K=32$ but separates intended from
wrong in all three
families at $K=512$; the across-family min-statistic interval is
$[+.003,+.048]$.  Intended-versus-reference predictive utility separates in
pairwise and sparse but not additive at $K=512$, so CARTE supports
content sensitivity for correspondence knowledge at that budget, not an all-budget
predictive-utility claim. Measurement knowledge is
mixed and relation knowledge is not statistically separated from zero under CARTE's
value/graph implementation. CARTE is therefore not presented as a full TransTab
replication.
